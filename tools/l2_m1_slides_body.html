Eigenvalues and eigenphases

An eigenvector of a gate U is a state that U only multiplies by a number:

U|ψ⟩ = λ|ψ⟩

A unitary gate keeps lengths, so |λ| = 1 and we can write λ = e2πiφ with 0 ≤ φ < 1. The number φ is the eigenphase.

What are the eigenphases of the Hadamard gate H? (Hint: H·H = I.)

0 and 1/2. Since H·H = I, each eigenvalue λ satisfies λ² = 1, so λ = +1 or −1: e2πi·0 and e2πi·(1/2). The eigenvectors are cos(π/8)|0⟩ + sin(π/8)|1⟩ and the state orthogonal to it.

The quantum Fourier transform

The QFT on n qubits applies the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) to the amplitudes. A basis state |j⟩ becomes

QFT|j⟩ = (1/√2ⁿ) Σk e2πi·j·k/2ⁿ |k⟩
  • Every output amplitude has the same size, 1/√2ⁿ.
  • The phase of |k⟩ grows by j/2ⁿ of a full turn from one k to the next: the number j becomes a rate of turning.
  • The QFT is unitary, so the inverse QFT turns the phase pattern back into |j⟩.

The QFT circuit for three qubits

The controlled phase between qubits k and j has angle π/2j − k. In code: qc.cp(math.pi / 2**(j - k), k, j).

Module 1 · The QFT and phase estimation7

Why the swaps?

Each stage puts one bit of the answer's phase pattern on a qubit, but the circuit fills the qubits in reverse order: the highest qubit ends up holding what belongs on qubit 0.

The swaps at the end reverse the order, qubit i with qubit n − 1 − i. Without them the circuit is the DFT with its output bits reversed.

A common mistake

Leaving out the swaps. The lab's check cell recognises this case and says so. Writing the angles with a minus sign gives the inverse QFT, which it also recognises.

About n²/2 gates for 2ⁿ amplitudes. The classical fast Fourier transform needs about n·2ⁿ steps. But the QFT's output is a quantum state: you cannot read all 2ⁿ amplitudes, only measure it. It helps only inside an algorithm that needs one measurement, such as phase estimation.

Phase kickback, again

Put the target in an eigenvector |ψ⟩ of U, and the control in |+⟩. A controlled-U gives

(|0⟩ + e2πiφ|1⟩)/√2 ⊗ |ψ⟩

The target is unchanged; the eigenphase has moved onto the control qubit. This is the same kickback as in Bernstein-Vazirani (Level 1, Module 6), now with any phase instead of a sign.

Powers of U

U2ᵏ has eigenphase 2ᵏ·φ. Counting qubit k controls U2ᵏ, so it picks up e2πi·2ᵏφ. For the phase gate this is one gate: cp(2π·φ·2ᵏ, k, t).

Module 1 · The QFT and phase estimation11

Why it works

After the controlled powers, the counting register holds

(1/√2ᵗ) Σj e2πi·φ·j |j⟩

Compare with QFT|x⟩ = (1/√2ᵗ) Σj e2πi·x·j/2ᵗ|j⟩: it is the QFT of |x⟩ with x = φ·2ᵗ.

  • If φ·2ᵗ is a whole number, the inverse QFT gives exactly |φ·2ᵗ⟩: every shot returns m = φ·2ᵗ.
  • Otherwise it gives a peak at the nearest whole numbers: the closest one with probability at least 4/π² ≈ 0.405.

An exact case: the T gate

The T gate's eigenphase on |1⟩ is 1/8 = 0.001 in binary. It fits exactly in 3 bits.

With 3 counting qubits: 1,000 of 1,000 shots give 001, so m = 1 and the estimate is 1/8 = 0.125.

φ = 0.7 with 5 counting qubits. Which result m comes up most often, and what estimate does it give?

φ·32 = 22.4, so the nearest whole number is m = 22 and the estimate is 22/32 = 0.6875 (error 0.0125). It is the most likely result, with probability 0.573; m = 23 (0.71875) comes next.

Where phase estimation is used

Shor's algorithm

The period of a·x mod N appears as an eigenphase s/r. Phase estimation reads it, and continued fractions recover r. Module 3.

Chemistry

The energy of a molecule is an eigenvalue of its Hamiltonian; phase estimation of e−iHt reads it. It needs fault-tolerant hardware; Module 5's VQE works on today's machines.

Counting and more

Phase estimation of Grover's operator counts solutions (quantum counting), and it underlies many other algorithms.

When you watch the video clips, compare each figure's qubit order with Qiskit's: in Qiskit, qubit 0 is the rightmost bit of a result.

Summary

  • A unitary's eigenvalues are e2πiφ; φ is the eigenphase.
  • QFT|j⟩ spreads |j⟩ into equal amplitudes whose phases turn at rate j/2ⁿ.
  • The QFT circuit: H, controlled phases π/2j − k, and swaps; about n²/2 gates.
  • Phase estimation: kickback from controlled U2ᵏ, then the inverse QFT, then measure m; φ ≈ m/2ᵗ.
  • Exact when φ has t binary digits; otherwise the closest estimate has probability at least 4/π².
