What a qubit platform must deliver

Qubits that last

Two well-defined states that keep their phase (T2) much longer than a gate takes.

Gates and readout

A universal gate set and a measurement, all with small errors.

Room to grow

Many qubits that can be prepared, wired, controlled and connected, ideally made in a factory.

These are DiVincenzo's criteria (2000), in short. Every platform meets them; they differ in how well, how fast and at what scale.

The numbers on a spec sheet

qubitshow many, and which pairs can interact (connectivity)
e1, e2error per single-qubit and two-qubit gate (0.001 = 99.9% fidelity)
e_roreadout error, or SPAM error (preparation and measurement)
T1, T2energy relaxation and dephasing times (Module 1)
gate timehow long an operation takes; with T2 it says how many gates fit

Always ask what kind of number it is: median over a chip, mean with all qubits running, best pair, or a bound.

Superconducting circuits

  • A transmon is a tiny circuit: a capacitor and a Josephson junction (two aluminium layers with a thin oxide between them). Its two lowest energy levels, a few GHz apart, are the qubit.
  • Made with lithography on silicon or sapphire wafers; cooled to about 15 mK in a dilution refrigerator; controlled with microwave pulses.
  • Fast: a CZ on ibm_boston takes /*BOSTON_T2Q*/ ns. Median CZ error /*BOSTON_E2*/, T2 /*BOSTON_T2*/ µs (calibration of 17 Apr 2026). Google Willow: mean CZ error 0.33% with all 105 qubits running.
  • Fixed wiring: each qubit talks only to its neighbours (3 or 4).

Trapped ions

  • Single charged atoms held by electric fields in a vacuum chamber, above a chip-like trap. The qubit is two hyperfine levels of each ion.
  • Lasers or microwaves drive the gates; ions are moved around the trap so that any two can interact.
  • Quantinuum Helios (98 barium ions, 2025): two-qubit error 7.9 × 10⁻⁴, single-qubit 2.5 × 10⁻⁵, among the best published.
  • Slow: about 70 µs per two-qubit gate on Helios, 600 µs on IonQ Aria, against tens of nanoseconds on a superconducting chip.
Module 5 · Quantum hardware and careers6

Neutral atoms

  • Uncharged atoms (rubidium, caesium) held in arrays of laser beams, "optical tweezers", in a vacuum cell.
  • Two-qubit gates excite atoms to large Rydberg states for a few hundred nanoseconds: 99.52% fidelity on up to 60 atoms in parallel (Harvard, MIT, QuEra, 2023).
  • Scale: 6,100 caesium atoms trapped with 12.6 s coherence (Caltech, 2025), without gates yet.
  • Atoms can be moved to change connectivity; moving and reading out atoms is slow, and atoms are sometimes lost.
Module 5 · Quantum hardware and careers7

Silicon spin qubits: transistors that hold one electron

  • A gate electrode traps a single electron in a quantum dot a few tens of nanometres across, under gates much like a transistor's. Its spin, up or down in a magnetic field, is the qubit.
  • Intel's Tunnel Falls: 12 dots made on 300 mm wafers with EUV lithography; 96% of the devices tested reached the single-electron regime.
  • Diraq and imec (Nature, 2025): devices from a 300 mm foundry line with one- and two-qubit fidelities above 99%. A remaining error source is silicon-29, whose nuclear spins disturb the electron: purified silicon-28 helps.
  • Tiny and factory-made, but the largest arrays have only about a dozen qubits so far.
Module 5 · Quantum hardware and careers8

Photons

  • Qubits carried by single photons or squeezed light in waveguides on silicon-photonics chips. Most of the hardware runs at room temperature; the photon detectors are cooled.
  • PsiQuantum (Nature, 2025): single-qubit preparation and measurement 99.98%, two-qubit fusion 99.22%, not counting photon loss.
  • Xanadu Aurora (Nature, 2025): 35 chips in four racks, 12 qubit modes per clock cycle, about 14 dB of loss end to end.
  • Loss is the main error: a lost photon is a lost qubit.

Pause and predict

Device A reports a median two-qubit error of 0.2%. Device B advertises its best pair at 0.1%, with a median of 0.4%. Which do you expect to run a 50-qubit circuit with 300 two-qubit gates better?

Device A. A 50-qubit circuit uses many pairs, so the median is the fair number: 0.998300 ≈ 0.55 for A against 0.996300 ≈ 0.30 for B. The best pair, 0.999300 ≈ 0.74, describes one pair, not the circuit.

Who builds quantum computers?

A survey of 57 quantum companies (Hughes and colleagues, IEEE Transactions on Education, 2022) listed 19 kinds of jobs, grouped here into three areas:

Hardware

device and component engineers, cryogenics, control systems, circuit design, photonics, test and measurement, technicians, experimental physicists

Software

software developers, algorithm developers, error-correction scientists, data scientists, computational chemists

Business

applications and solutions architects, sales and marketing, technical support

Most of the skills the companies rated important were not quantum: software development, electronics, circuit design, modelling, device testing.

Where semiconductor skills fit

  • Superconducting and silicon spin chips are made in cleanrooms with lithography, deposition, etching and metrology.
  • Silicon spin qubits come off 300 mm production lines; testing them uses wafer probers cooled to about 1.7 K.
  • Every platform needs microwave and RF engineering, cryogenics or vacuum, lasers and optics, and control electronics.
  • Technicians fabricate, assemble, test, operate and maintain these systems. QED-C's Guide to Building a Quantum Technician Workforce (2023) notes that companies often hire them from fields with shared skills: microelectronics, semiconductors and photonics.

Summary

  • Five platforms, one set of requirements: long-lived qubits, accurate gates and readout, and a path to scale.
  • Read every figure for what it is; compare medians with medians.
  • Two-qubit errors set the depth of a circuit: about 0.69/e2 gates halve the chance of an error-free run.
  • Coherence counts relative to gate time, and speed matters on its own.
  • Silicon spin qubits bring quantum computing into semiconductor fabs, and most quantum jobs need skills that are not quantum.
