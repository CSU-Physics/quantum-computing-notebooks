The hybrid loop

/*LOOP*/

The quantum computer only prepares states and estimates expectation values; the optimizer runs on an ordinary computer. Short circuits, many runs: a good match for today's noisy hardware.

Module 5 · Variational algorithms3

The variational principle

For any normalized state |ψ⟩ and Hamiltonian H with lowest eigenvalue E₀,

⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩ ≥ E₀

Write |ψ⟩ = Σ cₖ|Eₖ⟩ in the eigenbasis: ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩ = Σ |cₖ|² Eₖ, a weighted average of energies, so it can never be below the smallest one.

So: lower is always better, and the energy found is an upper bound on the true ground energy. Equality needs the ansatz to reach the ground state.

Module 5 · Variational algorithms6

The Estimator primitive

One call, many expectation values. Each PUB (primitive unified bloc) is a tuple:

(circuit, observable, parameter_values)
  • estimator.run([(qc, H2, theta)]).result()[0].data.evs is ⟨ψ(θ)|H₂|ψ(θ)⟩.
  • A list of observables gives one value each; a 2-D array of parameter values gives one value per row.
  • StatevectorEstimator computes exact values; on hardware, the Estimator measures with shots, so each value has a standard error.

A parameterized ansatz

/*ANSATZ*/

theta = ParameterVector("θ", 2 * layers); layer 1 is two RY gates; each further layer adds a CNOT and two more RY gates. One circuit serves every step: the Estimator fills in the angles.

The structure of Qiskit's real_amplitudes(2, reps=layers − 1). Real amplitudes are enough here, because the ground state of H₂ has real amplitudes.

Module 5 · Variational algorithms8

Classical optimizers

  • COBYLA (in SciPy) is gradient-free: it builds simple local models of the cost from its evaluations. Each evaluation is one Estimator run.
  • Gradient-based optimizers need derivatives, which cost two extra runs per angle (the parameter-shift rule).
  • Local optima: a local optimizer finds the bottom of the valley it starts in. Better starting points and several starts help.
  • Barren plateaus: in large, random-looking circuits the cost landscape becomes almost flat, and no optimizer can find a direction.

Pause and predict

With one layer, COBYLA stops 20 millihartree above the exact energy. Would running it ten times longer, or from other starting angles, get it to chemical accuracy?

No. With one layer there is no CNOT, so the ansatz can only make product states, and the best product state is |01⟩ at −1.836968 hartree. The exact ground state is about 0.994|01⟩ − 0.112|10⟩, which is entangled. The limit is the ansatz, not the optimizer: one CNOT more reaches the exact energy.

Max-Cut as a Hamiltonian

Split the nodes into two groups to cut as many edges as possible. For edge (i, j), ZiZj = +1 if both nodes are in the same group and −1 if the edge is cut:

HC = Σedges ZiZj,   cut = (|E| − ⟨HC⟩) / 2

The maximum cut is the lowest energy of HC. Course graph: 4 nodes, 5 edges; brute force over 16 splits gives a maximum cut of /*MAXCUT*/, for 0101 and 1010.

The QAOA circuit

/*QAOA_CIRCUIT*/

Start in the equal superposition; each layer applies the cost step e−iγHC (an rzz(2γ) on every edge) and the mixer step e−iβΣX (an rx(2β) on every qubit). p layers have 2p angles.

QAOA is inspired by slow (adiabatic) evolution from the equal superposition, the lowest-energy state of −ΣX, to the ground state of HC; with many layers it approaches that evolution.

With one layer and the best angles, the expected cut is /*QC*/ out of 4, and a maximum cut comes out /*P1*/ of the time. What do you expect from two layers, and what does it cost?

Two layers reach an expected cut of /*Q2*/, and a maximum cut comes out /*P2*/ of the time. The cost: twice as many angles for the optimizer, and twice as many rzz gates, each of which needs two-qubit gates on hardware (Module 4), so more noise.
Module 5 · Variational algorithms15

VQE and QAOA in practice

  • Measurement cost: on hardware every expectation value comes from shots; a molecule with thousands of Pauli terms needs many circuits per step.
  • Noise: the variational principle holds for the exact energy; noise and shot error can push an estimate below it, so mitigation (Module 4) matters.
  • Scale: IBM's QAOA tutorial runs Max-Cut on a 100-node graph (100 qubits); whether it beats the best classical methods is an open research question.
Module 5 · Variational algorithms16

Summary

  • Hamiltonians are sums of Pauli strings; the Estimator gives ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩ = Σ cₖ⟨Pₖ⟩.
  • Variational loop: ansatz on the quantum computer, optimizer on a classical one; the exact energy never goes below E₀ (an estimate from shots or a noisy computer can).
  • The ansatz limits what VQE can reach: for H₂, one CNOT made the difference between 20 millihartree and exact.
  • QAOA: HC = ΣZiZj, rzz cost steps and rx mixers; starting points matter, and more layers give better cuts.
