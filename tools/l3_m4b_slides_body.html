Why simulate with qubits?

  • The state of N spins has 2ᴺ amplitudes: 16 for 4 spins, about 10¹⁵ for 50. Classical exact simulation runs out of memory near 50.
  • A quantum computer stores the state in N qubits and applies the evolution as gates. This was Feynman's original reason for quantum computers.
  • Approximate classical methods (tensor networks, for example) handle many cases, and quantum results must be checked against them.

In this track the chain is small enough to compute exactly, so every error can be measured.

The transverse-field Ising chain

/*CHAIN_FIG*/
H = −J (Z₀Z₁ + Z₁Z₂ + Z₂Z₃) − h (X₀ + X₁ + X₂ + X₃)
  • Neighbours prefer to align along Z (coupling J = 1); the field h tries to turn every spin along X.
  • The chain is open: 3 ZZ terms, 4 X terms. In a Qiskit label qubit 0 is the rightmost character: Z₀Z₁ is "IIZZ".

Trotter steps

The ZZ part and the X part of H do not commute, so e−iHt is not a product of simple gates. But for a short time dt:

e−iH dt ≈ e+iJ dt Σ ZᵢZᵢ₊₁ · e+ih dt Σ Xᵢ
  • Each factor is a product of commuting gates: RZZ(−2J dt) on each pair, RX(−2h dt) on each spin.
  • Repeat n = t/dt times. The error of each step comes from the commutator of the two parts and is of order dt².
  • Over n steps the errors add up to order t · dt: smaller steps, more gates.

One step as a circuit

/*STEP_FIG*/

RZZ(θ) = e−iθ ZZ/2 and RX(θ) = e−iθ X/2, so the angles are −2J dt and −2h dt. On IBM hardware each RZZ is usually built from two CNOT or CZ gates around a Z rotation, on neighbouring qubits.

Pause and predict

Each RZZ gate now has a 0.5% two-qubit error. Will doubling the number of Trotter steps always make the result better?

No. More steps reduce the Trotter error but add more noisy gates. The total error falls, reaches a minimum and rises again. In the lab the first-order step does best with /*B1N*/ steps (error /*B1E*/) and the second-order step with /*B2N*/ steps (error /*B2E*/).

On real hardware

  • The best schedules above use 15 to 18 RZZ gates, about 30 to 36 CNOTs: fine for four spins today.
  • IBM's 2023 "utility" experiment (Kim et al., Nature 618, 500) ran Trotter circuits of this kind for a 127-spin Ising model, up to 60 layers of two-qubit gates, with zero-noise extrapolation.
  • For its largest circuits no exact answer existed; approximate classical methods were the only check.

In the capstone you can run a 2- or 3-spin chain with mitigation, on a noisy simulator and optionally on hardware.

Summary

  • Trotter steps approximate e−iHt by alternating short evolutions of the parts of H, built from RZZ and RX gates.
  • First-order error shrinks in proportion to the step; a symmetric second-order step shrinks it much faster for the same two-qubit gates.
  • On noisy hardware the best number of steps balances Trotter error against gate noise: /*B2N*/ second-order steps here.
  • Small chains can be checked exactly; large chains are why we want quantum computers.
