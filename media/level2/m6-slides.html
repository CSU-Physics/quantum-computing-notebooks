Mini-project: an experiment on a noisy quantum computer

Run one experiment on a noisy simulator, compare it with what the noise model predicts, and report the result with its uncertainty and its limits.

SemiAcademy · ISEP-CSUCC BY 4.0

By the end of this module you can

  • describe a noise model: depolarizing errors on gates and readout errors on measurements;
  • compute the noise model's expected value and compare a sampled run with it by the 3σ rule;
  • give a measured fraction or mean with its uncertainty, and test whether two results differ significantly;
  • find how a design choice (iterations, counting qubits, layers, transpiler settings) depends on the noise;
  • say what an experiment does not show: its limits.

Course outcome 5: run an experiment on a noisy simulator and report its results, their uncertainty and their limits; running it on a real quantum computer is an optional extension.

Module 6 · Mini-project4

A noise model in three parts

  • Two-qubit gates (cx, cz, cp, rzz, swap): a depolarizing error with parameter P2. With probability P2 the two qubits become completely random:
ρ → (1 − P2) ρ + P2 · I/4
  • One-qubit gates: the same on one qubit, with P2/10.
  • Readout: a 0 is recorded as 1 with probability R, a 1 as 0 with probability 2R (an excited qubit can decay while it is measured).

Errors are attached by gate name. A gate on three qubits (ccx, mcx) would get no error, so the projects use only one- and two-qubit gates, as a real computer does.

Module 6 · Mini-project6

Two ways to use a noise model

Expected value: follow the density matrix through the noisy gates, then apply the readout errors. Exact, no shots: expected_probabilities(qc, p2, readout).

Sampled run: AerSimulator(noise_model=nm).run(qc, shots=4000) draws 4,000 results from the same probabilities.

Grover, marked 101, 2 iterations, course model: the expected P(101) is 0.6870 (ideal 0.9453).

Shot noise

A result with probability p, in S shots, comes out k times: a binomial number. The fraction f = k/S has mean p and standard deviation

σ = √( p (1 − p) / S )
shots S1001,0004,00016,000
σ for p = 0.6870.04640.01470.00730.0037

Four times as many shots halve σ. One run of 4,000 shots of the Grover circuit pins its success down to about ±0.022 (3σ).

Module 6 · Mini-project8

The course rule: 3σ

A measured fraction f agrees with an expected value p when |f − p| ≤ 3σ. A correct run falls outside only about 3 times in 1,000 (0.27%).

On a noisy simulator, p must be the noise model's expected value. Example, one run of the Grover circuit: f = 0.6783.

Module 6 · Mini-project9

Comparing two results

Two fractions from S independent shots each: the difference f₂ − f₁ has

σdiff = √( f₁(1 − f₁)/S + f₂(1 − f₂)/S )

Uncertainties of independent results add in quadrature, so a difference is less certain than either result. A difference above 3σdiff is significant.

Grover, course model: 1 iteration 0.6342, 2 iterations 0.6870. Difference 0.0528, σdiff = 0.0106 with 4,000 shots each: about 5σdiff, significant.

Module 6 · Mini-project10

How many shots does a question need?

To see a difference d at 3σdiff, with S shots for each result:

S > 9 ( p₁(1 − p₁) + p₂(1 − p₂) ) / d²

Grover at P2 = 0.015: 1 iteration 0.6007, 2 iterations 0.6180, d = 0.0173. That needs about 14,000 shots for each, not 4,000.

Close to a crossover, "which is better?" is an expensive question. The answer can be: the data cannot tell.

Module 6 · Mini-project11

Uncertainty of a mean

Some results are averages over shots of a quantity that varies: the mean error of a phase estimate, the mean cut of QAOA. Their uncertainty is the standard error of the mean:

SEM = s / √S

where s is the standard deviation of the quantity over the shots. Two means differ significantly when their difference exceeds 3 √(SEM₁² + SEM₂²): the same rule as for fractions.

QAOA, 2 layers, course model: expected cut 4.333; one shot's cut spreads by s ≈ 0.97, so 4,000 shots give SEM ≈ 0.015.

Module 6 · Mini-project12

Uncertainty that σ does not include

  • Choices made at random: the transpiler's seed changes the layout and routing. For 4 qubits at level 0, ten seeds spread by 0.011, more than the shot noise 0.007.
  • The computer changes: real calibrations drift; a run tomorrow can differ by more than its σ.
  • The model is not the computer: a 3σ agreement with a noise model says the run matches that model, nothing more.

σ from one run measures how well that run measured its circuit. Questions about a setting need repeats over everything that varies.

Module 6 · Mini-project15

Limits: what a noise model leaves out

  • Connectivity: real qubits have few neighbours; swaps add two-qubit gates (Module 4).
  • Errors that repeat: a gate that over-rotates by the same angle every time; such errors add up instead of averaging out.
  • Crosstalk and leakage: neighbouring qubits disturb each other; a qubit can leave the states |0⟩ and |1⟩.
  • Drift: every qubit and gate has its own error, and it changes between calibrations.

Every experiment has limits. Saying what a result does not show is part of reporting it.
