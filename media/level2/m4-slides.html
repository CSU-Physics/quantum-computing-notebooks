Running on real hardware

A circuit on paper is not yet a circuit a quantum computer can run. The transpiler rewrites it for the real device, and every two-qubit gate it adds costs accuracy.

From your circuit to an ISA circuit

A quantum computer accepts only its own instruction set architecture (ISA): its native gates, on pairs of qubits that are actually connected. The transpiler gets there in six stages.

In Qiskit: pm = generate_preset_pass_manager(backend=backend, optimization_level=3), then isa = pm.run(circuit). Stage names from the IBM Quantum documentation, "Introduction to transpilation".

Module 4 · Running on real hardware3

Native gates

IBM Heron computers such as ibm_pittsburgh run four gates: cz (the only two-qubit gate), rz(θ), sx (the square root of X) and x. Every other gate is rewritten in these.

rz costs nothing: it is done by changing the phase of later pulses ("virtual Z"). The cz gates are the slow and error-prone ones.

Examples

H = RZ(π/2) · SX · RZ(π/2), up to a global phase.

CX = H on the target, CZ, H on the target.

SWAP = 3 CX = 3 CZ plus single-qubit gates.

A Toffoli (ccx) needs 6 CZ if all three qubits are connected, 7 if they sit in a line.

Connections: the heavy-hex lattice

FakePittsburgh has 156 qubits and 176 connected pairs. Each qubit has at most three neighbours: the lattice is made of hexagons with an extra qubit on every edge.

A cz can act only on a connected pair. The lattice has no triangles, so three qubits can never all be neighbours of each other.

The first rows of the device (qubits 0 to 59). Highlighted: qubits 0, 1 and 2, the trivial layout used at level 0.

Layout and routing

  • Layout chooses a physical qubit for each qubit of your circuit. A good layout puts qubits that interact next to each other, on qubits with small errors.
  • Routing adds SWAP gates when two qubits that must interact are not neighbours. Each SWAP costs three cz gates.
  • Grover's search on 3 qubits makes every pair interact. On heavy-hex the best possible placement is a line of three qubits, so some routing or longer decompositions are unavoidable.

In the lab (101, 2 iterations, seed_transpiler=11): level 0 starts on qubits 0, 1 and 2 (a line), and its routing SWAPs leave them in the order [1, 0, 2] at the end; level 3 chooses qubits [113, 114, 119], a line with 113 in the middle.

Pause and predict

Your circuit has a Toffoli on qubits 0, 1 and 2. On a computer where every pair is connected, it needs 6 CZ gates. On heavy-hex, can the transpiler find three physical qubits where it still needs only 6? Why or why not?

No. Six CZ gates need all three pairs to be connected, which would be a triangle, and the heavy-hex lattice has none. The best choice is a line of three qubits, where the transpiler (level 3) needs 7 CZ gates: one more two-qubit gate, and so a little more error, for every Toffoli.

Optimization levels 0 to 3

levelwhat it adds
0No optimization: translate and route only (used to characterize hardware). Keeps the trivial layout when it fits.
1Light optimization: a search for a better layout, and merging of single-qubit gates.
2Medium: a deeper layout and routing search, and cancellation of gates that commute. The default.
3High: level 2 plus resynthesis of two-qubit blocks (the KAK decomposition) and a few more passes.

Higher levels take longer to transpile and usually give shorter circuits. The layout and routing searches are random: fix seed_transpiler to get the same circuit every time. Source: IBM Quantum documentation, "Set transpiler optimization level".

Qubits differ

The level-0 qubits 0, 1 and 2 have cz errors of about 0.005 (three times the median) and T1 of 67 and 107 µs on two of them. The level-3 qubits 113, 114 and 119 have cz errors below 0.001.

T1: how long an excited qubit takes to decay. T2: how long a superposition keeps its phase. A cz takes about 0.09 µs and a measurement about 2.6 µs.

An error budget

If each two-qubit gate fails with probability ε₂ and each readout with εr, the chance that nothing fails is about

P(success) ≈ Pideal · (1 − ε₂)n₂ · (1 − εr)m

with n₂ two-qubit gates and m measured qubits. Because (1 − ε)ⁿ ≈ e−nε, the success rate falls exponentially with the number of two-qubit gates.

It leaves out single-qubit errors, decoherence while qubits wait, and errors that happen not to change the answer, so it is a guide, not a prediction.

Pause and predict

Level 3 gives 37 cz gates and 3 measurements. With the median errors ε₂ = 0.00155 and εr = 0.0045, and Pideal = 0.9453, what success rate does the error budget predict?

(1 − 0.00155)37 ≈ 0.944 and (1 − 0.0045)3 ≈ 0.987, so P ≈ 0.9453 × 0.944 × 0.987 ≈ 0.881. The noise model gives 0.892 (3,568 of 4,000 shots). With the actual errors of qubits 113, 114 and 119 the estimate is 0.907.

Error suppression and mitigation

Suppression

Change how the circuit runs: dynamical decoupling (pulses on idle qubits) makes fewer errors happen; Pauli twirling (random Pauli gates around each two-qubit gate) changes the kind of error, from coherent to random, without lowering its rate.

Mitigation

Run extra circuits and correct the results afterwards: TREX (readout errors), zero-noise extrapolation (amplify the noise, extrapolate to zero), and PEA and PEC (learn the noise and cancel it).

The cost

Mitigation needs more shots and circuits, and works on averages (expectation values) rather than single shots. It is not error correction, which protects each qubit with many physical qubits.

Awareness only: you will meet expectation values in Module 5. Source: IBM Quantum Learning, Quantum Computing in Practice, "Running quantum circuits" (Olivia Lanes).

Summary

  • The transpiler turns a circuit into an ISA circuit: layout, routing (SWAPs), translation to cz, rz, sx, x, and optimization.
  • Heavy-hex connections (at most three neighbours, no triangles) force extra two-qubit gates on circuits where every pair interacts.
  • Higher optimization levels cut depth and two-qubit gates; the success rate falls roughly like (1 − ε₂)n₂, and the chosen qubits matter.
  • Noise models from calibration data let you test this without a real computer; mitigation helps averages but is not error correction.
